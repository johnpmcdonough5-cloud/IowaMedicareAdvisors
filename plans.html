Medicare Advantage, explained plainly.

An overview of Medicare Advantage coverage and when you're able to enroll or make changes. For a comparison specific to your county and doctors, contact our office.

What is Medicare Advantage?

Medicare Advantage (Part C) is an alternative way to receive your Medicare benefits through a private insurance carrier approved by Medicare. Plans are required to cover everything Original Medicare covers, and many include additional benefits.

Combined Coverage

Medicare Advantage plans typically combine hospital coverage (Part A), medical coverage (Part B), and often prescription drug coverage (Part D) into a single plan.

Additional Benefits

Many plans include benefits not covered by Original Medicare, such as dental, vision, hearing, or fitness programs, depending on the plan and carrier.

Provider Networks

Plans generally use a network of doctors and hospitals. Whether your current providers are in-network is one of the most important things we check together.

Medicare enrollment periods

When you can enroll in or change a Medicare Advantage plan depends on your situation. These are the most common enrollment windows.

Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)
A seven-month window around your 65th birthday — three months before, your birthday month, and three months after — to enroll in Medicare for the first time.
Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)
October 15 – December 7 each year. You can switch, join, or drop a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, with changes effective January 1.
Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA OEP)
January 1 – March 31. If you're already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare once during this window.
Special Enrollment Period (SEP)
Available outside the windows above if you qualify due to certain life events — such as moving, losing other coverage, or a change in Medicaid or Extra Help eligibility.
